01. Units & Measurement

Basics SI Units

Measurement is the comparison of an unknown physical quantity with a known standard quantity.

SI Unit of Length = metre (m)
SI Unit of Mass = kilogram (kg)
SI Unit of Time = second (s)

Other important SI units include ampere, kelvin, mole and candela.

02. Motion

Kinematics Important

Motion means a change in position of an object with time.

Distance: Total path travelled.

Displacement: Shortest distance from initial to final position, with direction.

Speed = Distance / Time
Velocity = Displacement / Time
a = (v − u) / t
v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as

03. Force & Laws of Motion

Newton Force

Force is a push or pull that can change the state of motion of an object.

Newton's First Law explains inertia.

Newton's Second Law connects force, mass and acceleration.

Newton's Third Law states that forces occur in action-reaction pairs.

F = ma
Momentum = mv

04. Gravitation

Gravity Free Fall

Every object attracts every other object due to gravitational force.

Near Earth's surface, acceleration due to gravity is approximately 9.8 m/s².

Weight = mg

Mass remains the same at different places, while weight can change with gravitational field strength.

05. Work, Energy & Power

Energy Numericals

Work is done when a force causes displacement.

W = Fd cosθ
KE = ½mv²
PE = mgh
Power = Work / Time

Energy is the capacity to do work. Power tells us how quickly work is done.

06. Sound & Waves

Waves Sound

Sound is produced by vibrating objects and normally requires a material medium for propagation.

Frequency determines pitch, while amplitude is related to loudness.

v = fλ
f = 1/T

07. Light

Optics Ray Diagrams

Reflection is the bouncing back of light from a surface.

The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.

Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection

Refraction is the change in direction of light when it travels between different media.

1/f = 1/v + 1/u

08. Electricity

Circuits Ohm's Law

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge.

I = Q/t
V = IR
P = VI
Q = It

Ohm's law relates potential difference, current and resistance.

09. Magnetism

Magnetic Field Electromagnet

A magnetic field is the region around a magnet or current-carrying conductor where magnetic effects can be observed.

Electric current can produce a magnetic field.

Remember: The magnetic field around a straight current-carrying conductor forms circular field lines around the conductor.

10. Heat & Temperature

Thermal Physics

Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference.

There are three major modes of heat transfer:

Conduction: Transfer through direct contact.
Convection: Transfer through movement of fluids.
Radiation: Transfer through electromagnetic waves.

🧠 Quick Revision

Scalar Quantity

A physical quantity having magnitude only.

Examples: distance, speed, mass and time.

Vector Quantity

A physical quantity having both magnitude and direction.

Examples: displacement, velocity and force.

SI Unit of Force

The SI unit of force is Newton (N).

SI Unit of Energy

The SI unit of energy is Joule (J).
